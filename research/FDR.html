Quantifying the deviation from equilibrium

In equilibrium, the arrow of time ceases to exist: the system becomes time-reversal symmetric. This is a very powerful tool for physicists—it allows us to write down the precise form of the probability distribution, and infer how the system will respond to external perturbations just by observing its random, undisturbed motion. The latter is called the Fluctuation-Dissipation Theorem, or FDT for short. As part of my Ph.D., I quantified how active systems—for example biological matter which stays out of equilibrium by constantly converting energy from one form to another—respond to external perturbations. I showed, together with my collaborator, how we can extend the FDT into a wider array of non-equilibrium systems. These results both give a deeper theoretical understanding of systems out of equilibrium, and could help engineer and control biological or otherwise active matter.
