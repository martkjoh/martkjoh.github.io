Pion Condensate: matter under extreme conditions

Quarks are the fundamental building blocks of matter, making up protons and neutrons, which, together with electrons, make up atoms. The theoretical model that describes quarks and the force holding atomic nuclei together (the strong force) is called Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). In my master's thesis, I extended the effective field theory for QCD at low temperatures, which is called Chiral Perturbation Theory. (When high-energy physicists say low temperature, they mean anything cooler than the first fractions of a second after the Big Bang or the collisions in the Large Hadron Collider: below roughly a trillion degrees Celsius.) Our results give a more accurate model of the thermodynamic properties of pions, kaons, and other mesons, particles made up of quarks and gluons and which hold the nucleus together. This helps us understand the early universe.
